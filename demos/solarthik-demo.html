The vision

Not a replacement for the grid. A smarter relationship with it.

Bangladesh's weather makes solar-only unrealistic. So the product isn't "go off-grid" — it's a decision layer that makes the hybrid choice legible, financeable, and automatic.

01 — ASSESS

One honest number

Real tariff slab, real roof, real seasonal generation curve — a feasibility report, not a sales estimate built to close a deal.

02 — FINANCE

EMI sized to the worst month

Priced off monsoon output, not the annual average — so the "savings beat your payment" promise never breaks in the one month it matters most.

03 — OPTIMIZE

The system decides, hourly

Solar, battery, or grid — whichever is cheaper in that hour — switched automatically, not left to guesswork.

Market Benchmark

This has been solved before. Just not here.

Three markets, three different answers to "how do you finance solar for people who don't have Tk 200,000 sitting around." Bangladesh has the raw ingredients for all three and a packaged product for none of them.

🇵🇰 Pakistan

Bank-originated EMI

Structure
Conventional or Islamic financing, 3–10 yr tenure, fixed installments + insurance
Ticket size
Up to PKR 5M (Bank Alfalah); multiple banks compete (Meezan, Faysal, JS, HBL)
Who lends
Commercial banks, directly
Pitch
"Your EMI replaces your bill — and it's usually lower"
🇮🇳 India

Subsidized national scheme

Structure
Reducing-balance interest, 6-month repayment holiday, PSU banks (Canara, BoB)
Ticket size
Up to ₹6 lakh residential; subsidy up to ₹78,000 credited directly against principal
Who lends
Banks, backstopped by a government portal + subsidy
Pitch
Centralized national portal makes the subsidy + loan feel official and safe
🇰🇪 Kenya / East Africa

M-KOPA — pure PAYGo

Structure
Small deposit + daily mobile-money micro-payments; IoT SIM remotely locks the unit on missed payment
Scale
5M+ customers, $1.5B+ credit extended, no prior credit history required
Who lends
M-KOPA itself — vertically integrated hardware + finance + collections
Pitch
AI credit-scores customers from payment behavior, not a bureau file
🇧🇩 Bangladesh — today

The gap SolarThik fills

What exists
Generic bank personal loans (Tk 1–40 lac, 12–60 mo); IDCOL green refinance, mostly institutional-scale
What's missing
No bank markets a dedicated retail "solar EMI" product the way Pakistan or India do
Named blocker
Financiers don't trust the feasibility numbers (IEEFA) — a data problem, not a capital problem
Opportunity
Be the feasibility + monitoring layer that makes an existing lender comfortable

What Bangladesh can borrow from each

FROM PAKISTAN

The bank-partnership EMI structure. Fits BD's documented-income urban segment and matches the ownership psychology that already exists around property here — better fit than a rental model for most customers.

FROM INDIA

Pairing formal finance with a trusted, centralized layer. BD doesn't have a strong retail solar subsidy the way India does — so the trust has to come from an independent feasibility assessment instead of a government portal.

FROM KENYA

Payment-linked monitoring and alternative credit scoring. This is the most transferable idea, because it directly answers IEEFA's named gap for Bangladesh: real generation and repayment data is what unlocks bank capital here — not a bigger loan fund.

Positioning

Where to start

The idea alone isn't the moat — a dozen-plus EPC and hybrid-solar players already operate in Bangladesh. The wedge has to be sharper.

WEDGE 01

Compliance concierge

Navigate the Dec 2025 mandatory net-metering rule for buildings ≥1,000 sq ft or load expansions past 10kW. Urgent, narrow, low-capital — most owners don't know they're already required to act.

WEDGE 02

Vendor-neutral advisory

Every existing ROI calculator is run by the company selling the hardware. An independent feasibility + monitoring layer solves a trust problem, not a math problem.

WEDGE 03

Residential mass-market

Most existing sophistication targets factories and commercial buildings. Homes — newly eligible for net metering — look comparatively underserved.

Lender, or packager?

🏦 Hold the loan book

  • Usually needs NBFI licensing + real capital in Bangladesh
  • Startup carries default and collections risk directly
  • Full margin capture per deal
  • Slow to launch; hard to do first

✓ Be the packager Recommended start

  • Route customers to an existing bank / NBFI / IDCOL refinance
  • Supply the feasibility case + monitoring data that de-risks the loan for them
  • Little capital required, no default risk carried
  • Builds the data asset that could justify becoming a lender later
PHASE 1

Prove the model

Feasibility reports + compliance concierge for the mandatory-solar segment. Referral partnership with 1–2 existing banks/NBFIs for EMI origination.

PHASE 2

Build the data moat

Ship the monitoring layer. Real generation + repayment data becomes the leverage to negotiate better lender terms and lower rates for customers.

PHASE 3

Consider vertical integration

Once the data proves the model, evaluate originating financing directly — the M-KOPA path, but only after the trust problem is already solved.
